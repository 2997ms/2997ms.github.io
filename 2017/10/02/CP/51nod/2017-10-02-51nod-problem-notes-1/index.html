51nod 1742 Happy Little Q

This is a great topic. . . No way. . . Maybe it’s because there are too few questions like this. I can’t do the derivation of expressions + inclusion and rejection. . .

Before I lose my memory. . To summarize.

The question requires the total number of interesting divisors. The definition of an interesting number is a perfect square number greater than 1.

At this time, consider the Möbius function, so the question requires:
$$
S(n) = \sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{d|i}(1-|u(d)|)
$$
What I want is
$$
S(b)-S(a-1)
$$
then there are
$$
S(n) = \sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{d|i}(1-|u(d)|) = \sum_{x=1}^{n}\sum_{d=1}^{\frac{x}{d}}(1-|u(d)|) = \sum_{x=1}^{n}F(\frac{n}{x})
$$

$$
F(n)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(1-|u(i)|)
$$

At this time, you can find that $F(x)$ represents the number of square factors that do not exceed $n$. At this time, I found that it can be solved by tolerance and rejection. . .
$$
F(n)=n-\sum_{i=1}^{\sqrt{n}}u(i)*(\frac{n}{i^2})
$$
The meaning of this formula is that there are n in total, minus all the numbers without square factors. Why can’t Rong Shui think of it later? He’s so stupid. . .
