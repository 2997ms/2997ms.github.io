Reading what I said over these two years: another two years that are hard to look at.

2021-10-10

In this age full of temptations, I notice that people around me—including myself—often rely on outside forces to discipline themselves: join a daily check-in group or pay for a course with a fixed schedule.

But external dependence does not work for long. A real change of state still requires stronger inner resources.

I finally fulfilled a long-standing promise: http://2997ms.com/2997ms_me was nearly done. Many parts were still empty because I had no work to show—very embarrassing. Precisely because it was incomplete, its purpose was not to summarize, but more to guide myself.

I will not say “I hope my future self can XXXX” anymore. I have said it far too many times and become numb to it. I do not even know what effect saying it has.

In a moment I will put together an article for my WeChat Official Account. I hope it goes smoothly.

Yet the sea always embraces everything; it truly turns decay into legend.

May I become wealthy enough to buy a sea of advancing and retreating tides.

The point of writing weekly reports is not to drift through life in a daze, but to move toward a goal.

Not now, not now. Ten years of black hair exchanged for blah blah.

2021-03-21

3.15 - 3.21

This week I finished two books.

In Those Sad Young People, Xu’s later writing was genuinely brilliant—his understanding of university and his criticism of society. I came to see him as an intellectual, and as an idealist.

The Book of Lost Things landed below my expectations. It was not as stunning as I had expected; its final few pages moved me a little, but overall it felt ordinary.

One film: Dead Poets Society. It needs little introduction—“O Captain! My Captain!” Many people see it as an idealistic film and praise the teacher. But the director’s meaning seems clear: reality ultimately crushes idealism. The teacher leaves the school; a student leaves this world. What remains for us to consider is what education itself gives us: should it reduce constraints and help us discover ourselves, or add constraints and make us accept the rules of reality earlier?

This is an eternal question: it has no answer, yet we keep arguing about it.

2021-01-28 22:09

Our generation lives in an atomized way. Late at night, we lie in bed, open our phones, and express every kind of opinion about society, while barely valuing our connections with the people around us—selfish and self-absorbed.

Looking through Zheng Shuang’s Weibo, the obvious impression is of a person with an “authentic temperament” who worries about social hot topics, loves her country, and follows current affairs. I believe she sincerely cared about these things and enjoyed other people’s gossip like we do. But in her own life, this apparently extended to not even wanting her children.

At the end of Feng Xiaogang’s Personal Tailor, there is a memorable scene: “Why? You were willing to donate one billion earlier, so why not the car?”

Ge You says: “Anything else is fine, but not the car. I really do have a car.”

Li Dan said something similar: “The truth of life is this: I dare curse the world, blaspheme Buddha and ancestors, and look down on everything under heaven—but I do not dare curse the neighbor upstairs who renovates every weekend.”

Train yourself as if you were training a machine-learning model.

I have found that, when I fall into a sad kind of life, I need to talk to capable people and bring my physical state to its best. But to improve my physical state, I still need a mind free from anxiety.

The reason for my decline is too much contact with entertainment, which makes it hard to escape. The remedy is to expose myself to more good things, or find a hardware-based way to block the paths to entertainment.

Another is to meet outstanding people and let them push me.

Reading motivational books is another method, and it works very well too.

Then a virtuous cycle forms; otherwise it is a vicious cycle that you cannot escape.

Troublesome things at work often turn into that vicious cycle.

At midnight I read Peak. Books like this contain a lot of filler, but their ideas are good and they can still spur me into action.

Formally, it seems nothing has changed: today and yesterday, this week and last, this year and last. Familiarity often loses its charm. Xu Zhiyuan said, “Only in unfamiliar environments do I feel that life has not been wasted.”

2019-11-26 23:58

A sense of powerlessness.

I spent an entire afternoon on configuration. It worked on everyone else’s computer, and I followed the documentation step by step, yet in the end I still could not add the index. The longer it went on, the more irritated I became, unable to focus on anything else. I just held on stubbornly, ate too much again that night. I have spent too many years in this state—deeply frustrated and powerless before myself.

This frustration is a sharp contrast to my usual fiery mood. When my mind is in good shape, I feel I can take on the world; when it is bad, as now, life pounds me into the dirt. My biggest change over these years is how much less I joke about or mock life. Often I make a cheerful joke about life, and then life cuts me down until I cannot laugh or cry.

I once sighed, “How I want to escape this life, how I want interesting days.” But where would I go after escaping? Where could I go? Leave this life and I still have no better choice. Like a struggling ball, I eventually fall back to the ground.

“Life does not return to its starting point at any moment because it grows in a spiral. In every cycle, you meet an upgraded version of some challenge again; that too is a sign of progress.”
